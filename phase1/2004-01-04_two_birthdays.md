> ### THE VEGA-ODUYA FILES
> *by Marisol Vega-Oduya*
>
> **BIRTHDAY TWINS: THE KIDS I USED TO COVER ARE GROWN, AND HOLLYWOOD IS WATCHING TO SEE WHAT THEY DO ABOUT IT**
>
> One day apart, same zip code, twenty-two years old apiece: Tyler Chapman turns the big two-two today, and tomorrow the town's golden son, Marc-Anthony Bullock, catches up to him. I've been writing about both of these boys since they had matching Nickelodeon lanyards, so indulge an old columnist a little perspective.
>
> Chapman is the industry's best-kept not-really-a-secret — reliable, likable, never once the reason a picture flopped, never once the reason anyone bought a ticket either. There are worse ways to spend your twenties. There are also, and I say this with real affection, better ones.
>
> Bullock is the more interesting case, and not just because his grandfather could buy the studio lot Chapman shoots on and still have change left for the commissary. Three generations of Donohue-Bullock pedigree, a résumé as long as his last name, and still — still — no adult role that's made this town forget the Disney logo. He turns twenty-two tomorrow night with, my sources tell me, "half of young Hollywood" descending on the family estate. I'll be watching the trades Monday to see if any of that star power rubs off on a casting decision somewhere. It usually takes one good yes.
>
> Notably absent from any of my calls this week: word on Arianna Cummings, who I'm told tested for a studio drama over the holidays that I won't name until the ink's dry — or isn't. Third time in two years this young lady's come close enough to touch it. I have a bad feeling about how that one's going to go, and for her sake, I hope I'm wrong.
>
> *— M.V.O.*

### III. THE SCENE

**FADE IN:**

**INT. THE BULLOCK ESTATE — POOL HOUSE — NIGHT**

*JANUARY 5, 2004 — 9:47 PM.*

*String lights. A folding table buried under catering trays. A cake the size of a car tire that reads HAPPY 22ND MARC-ANTHONY (TYLER TOO, WE GUESS) in looping icing. Forty-some people mill in the warm California night — industry faces, family, the unmistakable gravity of old money worn lightly.*

*At the center of it: MARC-ANTHONY BULLOCK, 22 as of two hours ago, laughing with his whole chest at something AMOND BAKER just said. AMOND, 21, is doing an impression of a casting director that is, by the horrified delight on everyone's face, dead-on cruel and dead-on accurate.*

**AMOND**
*(pitch-perfect, oblivious industry voice)*
"We just don't see you as a *lead*, Amond. We see you as — and I want you to hear how much of a compliment this is — a *tremendous* number two."

**TYLER CHAPMAN**, *22 as of yesterday, howls.*

**TYLER**
Say it slower. Let it hurt him properly.

**AMOND**
"A tremendous. Number. Two."

**MARC-ANTHONY**
*(wiping his eyes)*
Stop — I'm gonna throw up in my own cake —

**ESTHER SMILLEY**, *21, arrives with two plates, hands one to Marc-Anthony without either of them acknowledging the handoff — old muscle memory.*

**ESTHER**
You didn't eat at the actual party part of the party.

**MARC-ANTHONY**
I was working the room.

**ESTHER**
You were making Amond do the casting-director bit for the sixth time.

**MARC-ANTHONY**
That's networking, Esther.

*TYRESE AVERY, 22, jet-lagged and radiant in the specific way only arena headliners manage, drops into a lounge chair with the exhausted authority of a man who just flew four hours for cake.*

**TYRESE**
I want it on the record that I have a show in Phoenix in thirty-one hours and I am here anyway, because I am a good friend, and also because Coach Thibodeaux told me there'd be an open bar.

**MARC-ANTHONY**
There's an open bar.

**TYRESE**
Then my priorities were correct.

*HARMONY DIVINE, 21, precise even in party clothes, appears at Amond's elbow with a flute of champagne she hands him without asking. He takes it without looking — they've done this dance for two years and never named it.*

**HARMONY**
You were good tonight.

**AMOND**
*(genuinely thrown)*
The bit?

**HARMONY**
The bit. And also not the bit. You're better than "tremendous number two" and everyone in this yard except the people paying you knows it.

*A beat. Amond doesn't have a joke ready for that one. He just nods, and drinks.*

*Across the pool, REGGIE "COACH" THIBODEAUX, early 60s, weathered and warm, taps a fork against a glass. The chatter dies down.*

**COACH THIBODEAUX**
I've known this one — *(pointing at Marc-Anthony)* — since he was eleven years old and couldn't hit a mark to save his life. I've known this one — *(pointing at Tyler)* — since he showed up to an audition in his church clothes because nobody told him it wasn't Sunday. Twenty-two, both of 'em, one day apart, and I'll tell you the same thing I told 'em at eleven: the town doesn't care how talented you are. It cares what you do next. So. What are you gonna do next?

*Silence. Then, from the back—*

**ARIANNA CUMMINGS**, *21, standing a little apart from the group with a glass of red wine she has, notably, not touched.*

**ARIANNA**
Ask me. I'll tell you what "next" gets you.

*Everyone turns. Arianna wasn't supposed to say that out loud — you can see it land on her own face half a second after it lands on everyone else's.*

**MARC-ANTHONY**
*(gently)*
Ari.

**ARIANNA**
No, it's fine — happy birthday, both of you, I mean that — I just. I tested for that Weller drama last week. The one Marisol's been sniffing around.

**ESTHER**
And?

**ARIANNA**
*(flat, controlled, the practiced calm of someone who has said this exact sentence in the mirror already)*
And they went with the other girl. The one with the website. The fan page thing, with the guestbook, hundreds of thousands of hits a month apparently, and the studio wanted "someone who already has an audience built in," and I stood in that room and was, by every account including the casting director's own mouth, the better actress, and it didn't matter. Because I don't have a website with a guestbook.

*A long beat. This lands different than a party toast is supposed to land.*

**TYLER**
*(quiet)*
That's the third one.

**ARIANNA**
That's the third one.

**AMOND**
*(bitter laugh, no humor in it)*
Welcome to the club. Mine's just "the funny friend" instead of "the girl with no website." Different door, same wall.

**MARC-ANTHONY**
*(sets his cake down, all the joking gone out of him)*
That's not a wall. That's a wall somebody built on purpose, and somebody can un-build it.

**TYRESE**
Careful. That's the face you make right before you say something that changes everybody's whole year.

**MARC-ANTHONY**
I'm serious. I think about this constantly — the family, the money, all of it, and what's it actually *for* if not to build something that outlasts a casting director's guestbook nonsense. I want kids. I want a lot of them. And I don't want them growing up in a business where the only thing that gets you in the room is who already let you in.

**ESTHER**
*(soft, watching him the way she's watched him for twenty years — like she's the only one in the yard who isn't surprised by anything he just said)*
Careful, Marc-Anthony. That's a very big thing to want out loud at your own birthday party.

**MARC-ANTHONY**
*(looking right at her, just a beat too long)*
I know exactly what I want, Esther. I've just been quiet about most of it.

*Before that can go any further — and it is about to go somewhere, everyone in the yard can feel it — Coach Thibodeaux clears his throat loudly, mercifully, and raises his glass.*

**COACH THIBODEAUX**
To twenty-two. And to every door in this town that hasn't opened yet, and is about to wish it had.

*Glasses raised. A chorus of "TO TWENTY-TWO." Amond claps Marc-Anthony on the back. Tyler steals a piece of cake off Tyrese's plate and gets swatted for it. Arianna, for the first time all night, actually laughs.*

*And in the middle of the noise, unnoticed by everyone except the audience, ROBERT BULLOCK checks his pager, frowns, and murmurs to NATASHA BULLOCK:*

**ROBERT**
*(low)*
Reggie's assistant says some kid at Harvard emailed the office. Wants to know if Marc-Anthony would "be an early user" of some website he built over Thanksgiving break.

**NATASHA**
*(already half-turned back to the party)*
Tell him no. Our son doesn't need a website. Our son *is* one.

*She smiles, raises her own glass to her son across the yard. Robert pockets the pager. Behind them, the string lights sway in a wind nobody else feels yet.*

**FADE OUT.**
