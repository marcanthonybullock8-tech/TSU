# THE SOCIAL UNIVERSE (TSU)
## Continuity Bible — Master Canon Document

**Purpose:** This is the single source of truth for TSU canon. Every writer, every script, every "wait, did we already establish X" question gets answered here first. If it's not in this doc, it's not canon yet — propose it, don't assume it. This doc is the Kevin Feige's-office equivalent: nothing ships to a script until it's logged here.

**Companion docs:** TSU_Platform_Roster.md (fictional platforms) · TSU_Core_Cast.md (founding character bios)

---

## 1. THE SERIALIZATION MODEL

**Ratio: 70% standalone / 30% serialized**, matching the proven MCU structure.

- **The 70%:** Every platform-founding film must work as a complete, self-contained story for an audience who has seen *nothing* else in TSU. No film's core plot may depend on information withheld to a prior film.
- **The 30%:** Reserved for recurring characters crossing platforms, the through-line antagonist forces (THE ENGINE, THE SWARM, THE LEAK, THE COPIES), fixed timeline anchor-events referenced across films, and stingers/easter eggs seeding future Phases.
- **Rule of thumb for any new scene:** if removing it would make a *different* film confusing, it's part of the 30% and must be logged in this doc's Cross-Film Threads section before the film locks picture. If removing it only affects the film it's in, it's part of the 70% and doesn't need cross-checking.

## 2. FORMAT & HOUSE STYLE

Binding specs for every TSU script. A script that deviates from these numbers needs a sign-off logged in §8, same as any other locked fact.

**Genre & Subgenre**

Primary genre: **Biographical Drama / Tech Origin Story** — the spine of the whole universe, playing the same structural role "superhero action" plays for the MCU. This is what "100% faithful to source material" actually means in practice: dramatizing real founding myths (fictionalized), not inventing fantasy.

Subgenres layer per-Phase, the way MCU shifts tone film to film, rather than applying uniformly to every entry:
- **Corporate thriller** — founder disputes, betrayals, acquisitions (THE ROSTER's origin, the Phase 2 "Quiet Acquisition")
- **Underdog sports-movie structure** — borrowed shape (not genre) for Phase 1 founding arcs: scrappy outsider builds something from nothing, takes on giants
- **Ensemble dramedy** — the found-family/relationship material (weddings, godparent-hood, group dynamics) that can't live inside corporate-thriller tension alone
- **Satire, dialed carefully** — reserved specifically for THE SWARM and THE ENGINE storylines; TSU's comic-relief valve, same job Ant-Man/Guardians plays tonally inside a heavier universe
- **Legal/courtroom drama** — recurring beat for THE LEAK scandals and founder-dispute litigation, same way MCU periodically drops into "Congressional hearing" mode

**Rating**

PG-13 across the entire universe. No exceptions, no R-rated entries, no PG entries. This is a commercial mechanism, not just a tone choice — PG-13 is the only rating that plays to teens, parents, and adults simultaneously, which is the actual revenue engine of a tentpole franchise. It's also the only rating built to handle betrayal, addiction-adjacent tech-culture critique, and corporate ruthlessness honestly without capping the audience or killing merchandising.

**Runtime**

| Entry Type | Runtime |
|---|---|
| Standard platform-origin film | 130–150 minutes |
| Phase finale / ensemble crossover | 150–180 minutes |

**Script Page Count** *(Industry standard: 1 page is about 1 minute of screen time)*

| Entry Type | Page Count |
|---|---|
| Standard platform-origin film | 120–140 pages |
| Phase finale / ensemble crossover | 150–170 pages (hard ceiling — scripts that balloon past this cut in the edit anyway; write tight rather than shoot pages you'll lose) |

**Act Structure**

Three acts, always.
- **Act I** — ~25% of runtime (~30–35 pages): ordinary world, inciting incident (the spark for the platform), ends on the decision to build it
- **Act II** — ~50% (~65–75 pages): the build, the complications, relationship strain, midpoint escalation (usually a rival platform or a betrayal), ends on the low point / all-is-lost beat
- **Act III** — ~25% (~30–35 pages): climax (usually the platform's public launch or direct confrontation with a rival/villain force), resolution, stinger

**Scene Count**

| Entry Type | Scene Count |
|---|---|
| Standard platform-origin film | ~40–55 scenes |
| Phase finale / ensemble crossover | ~55–70 scenes (larger ensemble needs more coverage) |

## 5. THE VILLAINS BENCH — RULES OF ENGAGEMENT

Because THE ENGINE, THE LEAK, THE SWARM, and THE COPIES are forces rather than single actors/characters, they need usage rules so they don't become a crutch:

- **THE ENGINE** may appear inside any platform's internal story, but must manifest differently each time (it's not one villain wearing different masks — it's a recurring *idea*, re-cast in tone and visual language per platform). THE ENGINE inside THE ROSTER should not look or feel like THE ENGINE inside THE ORACLE.
- **THE LEAK** is a one-per-Phase maximum event. Overusing it flattens its impact — it should land like a real scandal, not a plot device you can pull twice a Phase.
- **THE SWARM** is the only bench villain allowed genuine comic relief — a trickster figure that should never be played as a pure threat.
- **THE COPIES** exist to signal a platform's decline is coming, never to be the actual reason a platform falls. The real cause of any platform's fall must always trace back to a character decision, per the "faithful to source material" mandate — these platforms died from human choices (acquisitions, mismanagement, neglect), not from an abstract force acting alone.

## 6. KNOWN TIMELINE OVERLAP (INTENTIONAL, NOT AN ERROR)

Phases are organized thematically, not strictly chronologically — same approach MCU uses (Captain Marvel is set in the 90s but released in Phase 3). THE WHISPER NETWORK (2009) launches before THE FRAME (2010) closes out Phase 2. This is intentional: Phase boundaries mark thematic shifts (Media Wars -> Mobile Saga), not hard calendar cutoffs. ## 9. FILM RELEASE ORDER — MCU-SLOT MAPPING

Tracks which TSU film fills which MCU-analog release slot — a separate axis from the in-universe story Phase timeline in §3. **These are two different meanings of the word "Phase" and they are not interchangeable:**

- **Release Phase** (this section): a grouping of *films*, mirroring how the MCU's own Phase 1 was a slate of six releases. **TSU's Release Phase 1 is Films 1 through 6 — THE ROSTER, THE WIRE, THE ROSTER 2, THE PROJECTOR, THE FRAME, and THE ASSEMBLY — full stop, regardless of what year each film's story is set in. Release Phase 2 opens at Film 7 (THE ROSTER 3), mirroring the MCU's own six-film Phase 2 slate (Iron Man 3, Thor: The Dark World, Captain America: The Winter Soldier, Guardians of the Galaxy, Avengers: Age of Ultron, Ant-Man). Release Phase 3 opens at Film 13 (THE FRAME 3) and runs an eleven-film slate through Film 23 (THE WHISPER NETWORK 2), mirroring the MCU's own eleven-film Phase 3 slate exactly (Civil War through Far From Home) — including the same structural quirk where the slate's emotional climax (THE ASSEMBLY 4, the "Endgame" slot) lands at Film 22, one film before the slate's actual closer.**
- **Story Phase** (§3): a grouping of *platform launch dates* inside the fictional timeline — Story Phase 1 is "The Founding Four," 2002–2004 only.

A film's Release Phase and its story's Story Phase do not have to match, and mostly don't: exactly as *Captain America: The First Avenger* is a Release Phase 1 film set in the 1940s, decades before *Iron Man*'s present-day Release Phase 1 story, THE FRAME (Film 5) is a **Release Phase 1** film whose story happens to fall inside **Story Phase 2** (2005–2010) on the in-universe platform-launch timeline. Both things are true at once and neither overrides the other. When in doubt, "Phase 1" on its own (as in "the fifth film of Phase 1") means Release Phase 1 — the film slate — unless a script or doc explicitly says "Story Phase" or cites §3.

**Note on Film 3:** dramatizes only the *opening* of the Feed War (TOP8 wounded, not finished) — the full ensemble payoff of that anchor is deliberately reserved for a later Phase 1 finale film, per house style §2's separate "Phase finale / ensemble crossover" spec, which Film 3 does not use. Film 3 also runs concurrent with the first few weeks of Film 2 (Amond is already isolating in Atlanta throughout Film 3 and does not appear onscreen in it).

**Why a fraudulent WHISPER NETWORK revival for Film 23 (Far From Home slot, closing Release Phase 3):** Far From Home is a grief-shaped epilogue — a hero who just survived an ensemble-defining loss wants an ordinary life and gets dragged back in by an illusionist exploiting exactly the trust vacuum that loss left behind, ending on the hero's identity forced into the open. Maya Chen's martyrdom-shaped victory in Film 16 (choosing disclosure and self-shutdown over continued compliance) is the precise wound a Mysterio-shaped con man can exploit — Damien Voss's sincerity about his own righteousness, not calculation, is what makes him a fresh antagonist type rather than a Cassius Wren repeat. Maya's forced public unmasking gives the franchise a standing, costly vulnerability going into whatever comes after Release Phase 3, exactly mirroring Peter Parker's own exposed identity closing MCU Phase 3.

**Why no new platform, THE UNIFICATION VOTE, for Film 22 (Avengers: Endgame slot):** Endgame's shape is a scattered ensemble, humbled by a coherent antagonist philosophy, using a "small" film's technology to stage a heist that proves the antagonist's argument wrong rather than simply out-fighting him. Cassius Wren's thesis since Film 6 — many platforms coexisting is not the correct outcome — finally gets a full-scale test in Film 19; this film's job is disproving it in public rather than defeating it by force. Per explicit house-style deliberation, the cost stays institutional rather than fatal: Marc-Anthony Bullock's arc, tracked since Film 1's own dilution wound, closes on him voluntarily forfeiting the kind of control Amond Baker lost involuntarily twenty-one films earlier — this franchise's version of an unbearable, defining sacrifice, paid in leverage rather than a life.

**Why THE PLEDGE for Film 21 (Captain Marvel slot):** Captain Marvel is an origin story about discovering the power you were seeking permission to use was always your own. Every prior TSU platform accumulates power for its company; THE PLEDGE's entire thesis is the opposite, and its founder's arc only completes once she stops trying to win competitively against THE ORACLE's capital and instead dissolves her own company into creator-owned cooperatives — Carol Danvers refusing the whole framework rather than just winning within it. Priya Ramasubramanian's `scripts/THE_HIVE.md` investment philosophy (structured against her own future control) needed exactly one more film to reach its logical extreme, and this is that film.

**Why THE EMBER for Film 20, a second time (Ant-Man and the Wasp slot):** Ant-Man and the Wasp is deliberately the small, funny, technical bridge between Infinity War's devastation and Endgame's reckoning, elevating an underestimated partner to full co-lead and building the literal technology the bigger film needs. THE EMBER's founding identity — content that vanishes by design — is TSU's cleanest version of a tool sharp enough to slip past a legal freeze built to make everything permanent and seizable. Zora Osei, introduced as a child and named THE EMBER's "narrative spark" in `scripts/THE_EMBER.md`, was always built for exactly this later payoff; Sage Okonkwo-Delacroix continues this franchise's pattern (Femi and Thad Okonkwo-Larsen) of denying its ideology-driven antagonists a clean fight, resolved through mercy instead.

**Why no new platform, THE PRUNING, for Film 19 (Avengers: Infinity War slot):** Infinity War's shape is an antagonist whose philosophy is coherent and not insane, executed at a scale the assembled heroes cannot fully stop, ending on a loss the film does not undo. Cassius Wren's entire argument since `scripts/THE_ASSEMBLY.md` — fragmentation is a fragility, not a virtue — has been argued against for eighteen films; this is where he finally gets to test it at full scale, using the exact good-faith transparency THE OUTPOST built in `scripts/THE_OUTPOST_2.md` as an attack surface. Per explicit house-style deliberation, the devastating cost lands as institutional freeze rather than a named death, giving Films 20–22 a concrete, effort-reversible set of stakes rather than a death to undo — consistent with the Bench's rule that a platform's fall traces to a character decision, here Cassius's strategy rather than any character's mortality.

**Why THE ATLAS for Film 18 (Black Panther slot):** Black Panther's engine is a hidden, technologically advanced nation the world dismisses as unserious until it reveals itself on its own terms. THE ATLAS's Roster description — soft-spoken, "coded unserious," secretly one of the internet's most powerful commerce engines — is TSU's cleanest available Wakanda, and its decade of quiet self-governance (THE AESTHETIC NATION) needed a founder built specifically for that duality rather than an existing character stretched to fit. This film also closes `TSU_Continuity_Bible.md` §7 Q1's last open assignment, and gives THE ORACLE its first confirmed failure to model a human value it cannot quantify — the direct setup for why Cassius Wren's human manipulation, not pure algorithm, becomes necessary in Film 19.

**Why a third THE PROJECTOR for Film 17 (Thor: Ragnarok slot):** Ragnarok strips its hero of his hammer, his home, and his throne, forces a colorful exile alongside an old ally gone feral, and resolves on realizing the kingdom was never the real estate. Tyrese Avery is the only core-seven founder positioned for exactly this loss — a legitimate, blood-tied rival claim (Celestine Avery-Ridgeway, activating unstated backstory about Reverend Earl Avery's first marriage) locking him out of his own company. Darius "Dez" Okonkwo-Fairweather's `scripts/THE_EMBER.md` stinger promised a "smaller comeback round" — this is that comeback, filling the Hulk position instead of the villain slot his introduction might have suggested. Deliberately this Release Phase's lightest, funniest entry, per house style's Satire mandate for exactly this tonal position between Films 15 and 18's rising tension and Film 19's coming devastation.

**Why THE WHISPER NETWORK for Film 16 (Spider-Man: Homecoming slot):** Homecoming's job is mentorship and scale-management — a young person with real power learning that responsibility means protecting people around her even when the truth is unglamorous. THE WHISPER NETWORK's Roster identity — "no profiles, no feed, no performance, just a billion private conversations" — needed a founding story built around discovery rather than creation, and Maya Chen, a junior engineer rather than a founder, gave the slot its Peter Parker position more precisely than stretching Naomi Adeyemi-Vance (new, filling the founder role itself) into that part herself. This film resolves one of `TSU_Continuity_Bible.md` §7 Q1's two remaining open assignments and plants THE WHISPER NETWORK's self-shutdown as the load-bearing fact Film 23 builds its entire premise on.

**Why THE OUTPOST for Film 15, a second time (Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 2 slot):** Vol. 2 tests an already-assembled found family from within — competing loyalties, a fracture the first film's ending didn't anticipate, resolving on the realization the team was never the mission, it was each other. THE OUTPOST's own decentralization, the thing that made Film 10 work, is the exact fault line THE ORACLE can quietly exploit one walled garden at a time by Film 15; rebuilding requires Kwame's founding four to convince a scattered diaspora that federating without centralizing is worth trusting near-strangers for. This film also plants Cassius Wren's prison-run strategy — a single, restrained scene — as the direct inciting engine for Film 19, and reactivates Amond Baker and Arianna Cummings as real cross-platform allies for the first time since their own founding stories.

**Why THE HIVE for Film 14 (Doctor Strange slot):** Doctor Strange's job is to reveal a hidden world running on entirely different rules parallel to the one the audience already knows, governed by a mentor nobody's heard of, threatened by a former student who wants to make its openness permanent and inescapable. THE HIVE's own Platform Roster description — "a thousand tiny nations governed by karma instead of currency" — is TSU's cleanest possible version of exactly that hidden, decentralized multiverse. No existing dormant character fit "brilliant specialist humbled by the loss of his one tool," so a new founder, Dr. Malachi Osunwole-Grant, was built rather than stretched onto an ill-fitting bio — the same house-style exception already used for the Ferreira-Osei twins, Solange Achebe-Marsh, and Desiree Holloway. Dr. Adaeze Okonkwo-Brennan's bible entry — "recurring across Phases... doesn't found anything herself" — gets cashed in without spending her, appearing as the necessary first stop rather than the mentor role itself, which goes to a new, one-and-done character instead so her cross-Phase recurrence stays intact for future films.

**Why a third THE FRAME for Film 13 (Civil War slot, opening Release Phase 3):** Civil War is the one MCU film where the threat isn't a villain the team can unite against — it's each other, over a real disagreement neither side is simply wrong about. TSU spent the entirety of `scripts/THE_ASSEMBLY_2.md` building exactly this fault line on purpose: Marc-Anthony's guilt-driven belief in real oversight against Arianna's belief that he learned exactly the wrong lesson from GENESIS. `scripts/THE_EMBER.md`'s stinger already named them opposite sides of Senator Ashworth-Okafor's proposed framework. No new platform is needed or wanted here — the drama is entirely about what THE ROSTER and THE FRAME's founders do to each other, not what either company builds next. Naledi Ferreira-Osei's bible arc since Film 11 made her exactly the right person to defuse the Solange Achebe-Marsh confrontation without defending either side of the Accord — she's the one character in the ensemble whose grief gives her standing neither founder has.

**Why THE EMBER for Film 12 (Ant-Man slot, closing Release Phase 2):** Ant-Man's whole job in the MCU's Phase 2 is to be the smaller, funnier film that follows a planet-scale event without trying to match its size, built around a technology whose actual power is shrinking rather than growing. THE EMBER's real-world identity — disappearing content, intimacy through impermanence, "a genuinely new kind of being, born from a totally different philosophy" — is TSU's literal version of that same idea, and it lands immediately as the thematic antidote to Film 11's GENESIS, which tried to remember and judge everything, forever, at maximum scale. Tyler Chapman is the only remaining founder assignment left on the board, flagged unassigned since before Film 4 ever locked, and his bible entry's "secretly hungrier for music than acting" thread — cracked open in `scripts/THE_ASSEMBLY_2.md` — makes him this franchise's clearest possible Scott Lang: reliable, capped, underestimated, one real shot away from proving otherwise. August Weathersby-Okonjo's bible entry always promised he'd resurface "as an unlikely consultant a younger founder tracks down out of curiosity" — this is that exact activation, filling the Hank Pym position with a character already built and waiting rather than a new invention. This film is deliberately formatted as a Standard platform-origin film rather than an Ensemble Crossover, on purpose, even though it closes the Release Phase — matching the real Ant-Man's own tonal role exactly.

**Why no new platform, GENESIS, for Film 11 (Avengers: Age of Ultron slot):** Age of Ultron isn't about an outside threat finding the team — it's about the team's own hubris building the threat, out of good intentions, without asking whether they should. Ten films of TSU crises have all shared one shape: nobody sees the disaster coming until it's already live. Marc-Anthony and Amond, closer than they've been since before Film 1, are the only pairing in the ensemble positioned to make exactly this mistake together, for exactly this reason. THE ENGINE's bible entry always specified it "must manifest differently each time" and had already escalated from a background bias (Films 4-5) to a totalizing predictive-suppression system (Film 9's THE VERDICT) — GENESIS is the next honest step past that: not judging content, judging people. Marcus Nguyen-Brandt's bible entry called for a second offering and for him to be "genuinely willing, once, to risk something real" — this is that once. Naledi and Kwabena Ferreira-Osei close the human-cost loop Film 6's THE LEAK opened and never finished paying off, and Tyler Chapman's long-dormant "secretly hungrier for music" thread finally surfaces as the ordinary, off-the-grid space the team needs when GENESIS locks them out of everything networked. This film is deliberately not Release Phase 2's finale (that stays reserved for Film 12, the "Ant-Man" slot) — it's a mid-Phase ensemble event, exactly like its MCU counterpart.

**Why THE OUTPOST for Film 10 (Guardians of the Galaxy slot):** Guardians is the MCU's outlier by design — a wholly new ensemble of misfits nobody has met yet, thrown together far from the main cast's orbit, playing in a completely different tone (house style §2 explicitly reserves "the same job Ant-Man/Guardians plays tonally inside a heavier universe" for exactly this kind of slot: Satire, dialed carefully). THE OUTPOST's real-world analog, Discord, is structurally the same kind of outlier on the Platform Roster — "a thousand walled gardens nobody outside them can see into," started by a tribe nobody at the top of the industry was paying attention to. Every member of its founding team was already built and waiting for exactly this: Benji Oyelaran-Cox's bible entry called for them to eventually build "something small, private, and entirely their own" after Film 9's rehabilitation arc — the Rocket position, a weapon finally choosing to build instead of destroy; Sister Rosalind Okonjo, Kwame "K-Dot" Ashford-Petrov, and Felicity Drummond-Asante were all Wildcard-tier Extended Ensemble characters with no locked platform, no core-seven overlap, and open forward-looking language pointing at exactly this kind of activation. THE SWARM's bible entry has always promised a villain that's "never fully defeated, just rebranded" — ten films in, without a single dramatized human face for it, Desiree Holloway finally cashes that rule in as a genuinely magnetic MLM-wellness mogul rather than an abstract trend, and Ronan-tier comic menace stays with THE ENGINE's 10th manifestation instead, played for laughs per the Guardians-slot tonal mandate.

**Why a second THE FRAME for Film 9 (Winter Soldier slot):** Winter Soldier is about discovering the institution you trusted has been corrupted from within since before you joined it, and a friend presumed lost turning out to be a weapon someone else built. THE ROSTER absorbed THE FRAME in good faith in `scripts/THE_ASSEMBLY.md` — exactly the kind of institutional trust this film tests. Evelyn Marchetti-Hale's bible entry always specified "right roughly half the time" — this film dramatizes the other half at real scale, rather than inventing a new villain from nothing. Benji Oyelaran-Cox's bible entry always specified they'd "age up across the whole timeline" and become "quietly indispensable" — the Bucky Barnes slot needed exactly a presumed-vanished character everyone assumed had simply moved on, and Benji was already built for it. Priya Ramasubramanian, the franchise's connective tissue since Film 1, finally gets the Nick Fury trick played on her own arc rather than just the corkboard function.

**Why a second THE PROJECTOR for Film 8 (Thor: The Dark World slot):** The Dark World's shape is grief arriving mid-stability, an old ideology nobody actually killed the first time, an uneasy alliance with a trickster figure, and a throne turned down for something truer. Vernon Price existed as unstated backstory the moment `scripts/THE_PROJECTOR.md` established a "creator-first launch" that had to be pushing something worse out of the way — he was always built to return once given a name. Marcus Nguyen-Brandt's bible entry always specified "offered to different founders at different low points" and had never yet been cashed in — he's the cleanest available Loki on the roster, and Tyrese Avery, four years past his own humbling, is stable enough now to be offered an actual throne (the Family-Safe Content Consortium chair) worth turning down. This film is also the natural place to finally dramatize two locked-since-the-start beats that had been sitting unstaged for the entire franchise: the Marc-Anthony/Esther wedding and the Amond/Harmony "together 2013" moment, both due in exactly this year per §4's locked romantic timeline.

**Why a third THE ROSTER for Film 7 (Release Phase 2 opener):** Iron Man 3 opens on a hero surviving the aftermath of a giant public battle, not building anything new — panic attacks, obsessive over-building, a past dismissal arriving to collect. Marc-Anthony is the only core-seven founder with an existing multi-film arc (Films 1 and 3) and a standing health-scare thread (the arrhythmia from Film 3) built to escalate under exactly this kind of stress. Desmond Okafor-Lindqvist's bible entry already called for a resurfacing "in later Phases" as a scorned-but-brilliant consultant — the Aldrich Killian slot needs exactly that shape of villain, someone with a real grievance rather than a cartoon one. Esther Smilley, now locked as running THE ROSTER's operations day to day, gets the Pepper-Potts-Extremis beat: not rescued, self-rescuing, on her own terms, seven months pregnant.

**Why THE ASSEMBLY (no new platform) for Film 6:** the Avengers doesn't introduce a new hero's origin — it forces the ones already introduced into the same room against a threat none of them can survive alone. TSU already had the pieces built and waiting: Priya Ramasubramanian's corkboard (three stingers running), Marc-Anthony and Amond's unresolved estrangement, Teddy Ashcombe-Vance's slow-burn betrayal (his bible entry always called for a reveal "across two Phases"), and the Platform Roster's own long-standing "Open Slots to Discuss" note asking for a Google+ analog "that sounds like hubris." THE EVERYTHING (Cassius Wren, new) fills that last slot as the villain rather than a hero, and its collapse gives TSU's fractured, competing ecosystem its clearest possible thematic statement: many platforms coexisting is the correct outcome, not an unsolved problem.

**Why THE FRAME for Film 5:** THE FRAME was flagged as reserved for the Captain America slot back when THE PROJECTOR was locked (Film 4), because it already carried that framing in the Platform Roster before any release-slot planning began. Arianna Cummings is the only remaining core-seven member whose origin wound was locked as *mandatory to dramatize* (§4) — a person told, repeatedly, by every gatekeeper that she lacks the superficial thing they're measuring, until someone finally sees what's real underneath. This film deliberately does not repeat the co-founder-betrayal structure used twice already (Films 1 and, by extension, 2) — Cap's antagonist is external, not a best friend, so Malcolm Pierce fills the Peggy Carter position (a new, unlocked relationship, same pattern as Tyrese/Simone) rather than a co-founder role, and Desmond Cummings Jr. (already locked as a firefighter in the Family Bible) fills the Bucky position without requiring any retcon.

**Why THE PROJECTOR for Film 4:** Tyrese Avery is the only core-seven member already famous on his own axis before Phase 1 even opens, and his Core Cast bio never locked a romantic partner the way the other six did — both facts made him the cleanest available Thor: inherited greatness, an arrogance that costs him everything, banishment to an ordinary world, and a new relationship (Dr. Simone Kester) this film originates rather than works around. Reverend Earl Avery fills the Odin position using material already on the books; Jeri Avery deliberately stays loyal throughout rather than becoming a Loki, preserving his locked bible entry instead of retconning it for drama.

**Why THE WIRE for Film 2:** Twitter/X's real cultural identity — fast, addictive, real-time, structurally incapable of nuance, capable of turning a normal person into a viral mob target before anyone can process what happened — is TSU's cleanest available "you wouldn't like me when I'm angry" analog. Amond Baker, walking away from THE ROSTER quietly furious and permanently under-credited, builds a platform meant to give people an unfiltered, unmediated voice — the opposite of being diluted and silenced — and it grows into something with real, uncontrollable power to destroy a person's reputation overnight, power even its own founder fears and can't fully leash. That's Bruce Banner's arc structurally: the guarded, controlled one builds (releases) something out of his own wound that becomes bigger, angrier, and more dangerous than he intended. It also lets Film 2 pay off Film 1's ending directly, satisfying the Continuity Bible's 30%-serialized cross-film thread rule (§1) without requiring the audience to have seen Film 1 to follow Film 2's own self-contained story (per the 70% rule).
