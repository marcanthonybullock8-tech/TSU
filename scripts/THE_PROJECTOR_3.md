# THE PROJECTOR 3: RAGNARÖK
### TSU Film 17 — Release Phase 3, Film 5 — the "Thor: Ragnarok" release slot

---

## THE PITCH

**Platform:** THE PROJECTOR (YouTube analog, `TSU_Platform_Roster.md`, Phase 2), a third time — no new founding story. This film asks what happens when the platform's own physical/corporate infrastructure is destroyed out from under its founder, and whether "THE PROJECTOR" was ever really the servers and the boardroom in the first place.

**Why this is Thor: Ragnarok, structurally:** Ragnarok strips its hero of his hammer, his home, and his throne in the first act, forces him into a colorful exile with an old ally gone feral, and resolves on the realization that the kingdom was never the real estate — "Asgard is not a place, it's a people." Tyrese Avery losing control of THE PROJECTOR to a legitimate, blood-tied rival claim, getting locked out of his own admin access, and rebuilding around the creator community rather than the infrastructure is TSU's direct equivalent, tonally lighter and funnier than the Phase's surrounding stakes on purpose, per house style's Satire mandate for exactly this kind of slot.

**Who this film belongs to:** Tyrese Avery, returning a third time. **Celestine Avery-Ridgeway**, new, the Hela position — Reverend Earl Avery's estranged elder daughter from a first marriage never previously dramatized, holding a legitimate minority claim to THE PROJECTOR's founding paperwork and backed by THE ORACLE's capital. **Darius "Dez" Okonkwo-Fairweather**, returning from `scripts/THE_EMBER.md` as VAPOR's founder, fills the Hulk position — once dangerous, now reluctant, talked back into the fight. Jamal Kester and Dr. Simone Kester return in load-bearing support.

**In-universe timeline:** **June – November 2021** (Story Phase 4).

---

## LOGLINE

When an estranged half-sister backed by THE ORACLE's capital seizes legal control of THE PROJECTOR and locks its own founder out of the company he built, Tyrese Avery has to survive exile from his own platform long enough to realize the thing worth saving was never the servers.

## SYNOPSIS

Reverend Earl Avery, dying, tells Tyrese one thing he never told him while healthy: before Tyrese's mother, there was a first marriage, and a daughter from it, raised apart, resentful, brilliant. **Celestine Avery-Ridgeway** has spent the years since building a career in media law specifically so that when she finally came for what she considers rightfully half hers — the co-signed paperwork from THE PROJECTOR's earliest, most desperate founding days, `scripts/THE_PROJECTOR.md` — no court could say she didn't earn the standing to claim it.

She doesn't ask. She executes a hostile legal maneuver, backed by a capital injection from a THE ORACLE shell fund eager to convert THE PROJECTOR's "creator-first" architecture into pure algorithmic engagement farming, and locks Tyrese out of his own company's systems in a single afternoon. Jamal Kester is sidelined. Simone Kester is threatened with an NDA violation for standing too close to the fight. Tyrese, for the first time since the crash that opened `scripts/THE_PROJECTOR.md`, has absolutely no platform at all.

Cut loose, humiliated, and — this film's tonal signature — increasingly, helplessly funny about it, Tyrese ends up on the periphery of a chaotic underground creator scene that has nothing to do with THE PROJECTOR's polish: a "gladiator" circuit of unmonetized, unverified, occasionally illegal livestream battles running on salvaged infrastructure. Its reluctant king is **Darius "Dez" Okonkwo-Fairweather**, VAPOR's disgraced founder, who has spent the years since Film 12 furious, hiding, and — when provoked — capable of the platform's own equivalent of turning green: a self-destructive livestream meltdown that draws more traffic than anything he's built on purpose. Tyrese needs Dez's underground reach; Dez needs a reason to stop being furious for long enough to help someone else.

Celestine's takeover accelerates: she strips THE PROJECTOR's "creator-first" governance entirely, installing pure predictive monetization within weeks, gutting the family-content protections Tyrese built in his mother Coralee's memory (`scripts/THE_PROJECTOR_2.md`). Her enforcer, **Garrick Solano**, a THE PROJECTOR executive of long standing, executes the purge without complaint — until Tyrese, cornered and honest rather than triumphant, gives him a real reason to switch sides.

The climax is not a legal victory. Tyrese and Dez's underground alliance can't out-lawyer Celestine, and by the time the confrontation lands, THE PROJECTOR's original architecture — the specific servers, the specific corporate shell, the thing with Tyrese's name on the original 2005 filing — is unsalvageable, deliberately corrupted by Celestine rather than let go back to him intact. Tyrese's answer, the film's thesis: let it go. He walks away from the legal fight for the *company* and instead migrates the actual creator community — the people, the channels, the relationships — onto new infrastructure entirely, built collaboratively with Dez's underground network and Jamal's quiet technical work. THE PROJECTOR, the file cabinet and the servers, is gone. THE PROJECTOR, the community, is not. Celestine, having won the paperwork and lost the thing the paperwork was supposed to be worth, is left holding an empty shell — one THE ORACLE, unsentimental, immediately loses interest in.

## THE ANTAGONISTS

1. **CELESTINE AVERY-RIDGEWAY**, new, the Hela position — Reverend Earl Avery's estranged elder daughter, a legitimate legal claim and a real grievance (raised apart, resentful, erased from the family's public story) weaponized into a hostile corporate seizure. Not defeated in court; left holding a hollowed-out win.
2. **THE ORACLE**, financing Celestine's takeover as a low-cost bid to convert THE PROJECTOR's architecture to pure algorithmic monetization — its second confirmed proxy-financing move this Release Phase (after `scripts/THE_WHISPER_NETWORK_2.md`'s Meridian Sightlines pattern, though this film predates that reveal in-story), continuing the strategic pivot the Bench is tracking toward Films 19 and 22.
3. **GARRICK SOLANO**, new, the Skurge position — a THE PROJECTOR executive who executes Celestine's purge without complaint until Tyrese gives him real cause to switch sides at real personal cost, redeeming late rather than early.

## HOW THIS FILM CARRIES THE FRANCHISE FORWARD

- **Deliberately the Phase's lightest, funniest entry**, per house style's Satire mandate for exactly this tonal slot — a needed tonal reset between Film 15's fragile federation and Film 19's coming devastation.
- **Reactivates Darius "Dez" Okonkwo-Fairweather**, whose `scripts/THE_EMBER.md` stinger promised a "smaller comeback round" — this is that comeback, on very different terms than he expected.
- **THE ORACLE's proxy-financing pattern gets its second confirmed instance**, strengthening the strategic through-line `scripts/THE_ASSEMBLY_3.md` and `scripts/THE_ASSEMBLY_4.md` pay off at full scale.
- **"The platform was never the infrastructure" thesis** directly rhymes with THE OUTPOST's federation answer in Film 15 and previews the franchise's Release-Phase-3-finale argument that community, not company, is what actually survives THE ORACLE.
