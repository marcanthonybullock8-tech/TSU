# THE OUTPOST: THE DOWNLINE
### TSU Film 10 — Release Phase 2, Film 4 — the "Guardians of the Galaxy" release slot

---

## THE PITCH

**Platform:** THE OUTPOST — TSU's fictional 1:1 counterpart to Discord (per `TSU_Platform_Roster.md`, Phase 3 — "Started as a tool for one specific tribe and quietly became the internet's town hall of private nations... hidden in plain sight.") Fixed launch anchor: 2015, per `TSU_Continuity_Bible.md` §3 — honored exactly, no change requested.

**Why THE OUTPOST is the perfect Guardians of the Galaxy slot:** Guardians doesn't add a hero to the existing team — it introduces an entirely new corner of the universe, populated by people the audience has never met, who don't know each other, don't like each other much at first, and would never be described as heroes by anyone including themselves. Per `TSU_Continuity_Bible.md` §2, "Satire, dialed carefully" is explicitly reserved for THE SWARM and THE ENGINE storylines and is described as "TSU's comic-relief valve, same job Ant-Man/Guardians plays tonally inside a heavier universe" — this slot was written into the house style before this film existed. None of the core seven lead it. That's the point.

**Who this film belongs to:** A brand-new found family, assembled from the Extended Ensemble's dormant Wildcards. **Kwame "K-Dot" Ashford-Petrov** (the Star-Lord position — swagger, a hustle gone sideways, zero interest in fame and enormous interest in money). **Benji Oyelaran-Cox** (the Rocket position — prickly technical genius, one film removed from `scripts/THE_FRAME_2.md`'s redemption, finally building something that's entirely their own). **Sister Rosalind Okonjo** (the Groot position — a genuine nun, gentle, load-bearing, says less than everyone else and means more of it). **Felicity Drummond-Asante** (the Gamora position — guarded, sharp, a rival-dynasty former child star who joins for her own reasons and ends up being the reason the team survives).

**In-universe timeline:** **January 2015 – June 2015.** THE OUTPOST's launch is fixed at 2015 (Phase 3 closes) per `TSU_Continuity_Bible.md` §3 — this script locks the public launch to June 2015. None of these four are core-seven members, so no birthdate cross-checks against `TSU_Core_Cast.md` apply; ages are established fresh in this film (all early-to-mid 30s, consistent with their existing bible entries' timelines relative to the core seven).

## LOGLINE

A crypto hustler with a grabbed flash drive he doesn't understand, a disgraced hacker trying to build something that's finally his own, a nun who runs the best-moderated Discord server nobody's heard of, and a former child star who trusts exactly nobody get thrown together by accident, discover they're sitting on proof that the internet's most beloved wellness guru is running a nine-figure pyramid scheme, and have to build a place sturdy enough to protect it before she buys, infiltrates, or destroys them first.

## SYNOPSIS

Kwame "K-Dot" Ashford-Petrov has never once in his life turned down a hustle that smelled like money, which is how he ends up walking out of a very wrong meeting with a flash drive he was absolutely not supposed to have, thinking it's crypto wallet keys. It's not. It's four years of internal financial records belonging to **Desiree Holloway** — the wellness industry's most beloved face, host of a nine-figure "abundance mentorship" empire that is, provably, a pyramid scheme with extra crystals — handed off by a nervous internal accountant who got the wrong guy's contact info and doesn't yet know it.

K-Dot, in over his head immediately, calls the only person he trusts with a hard drive full of trouble: **Benji Oyelaran-Cox**, a few months removed from the very public, very humbling end of a very bad three years, currently building something small, private, and entirely their own — a little invite-only server for people who just want somewhere that isn't owned by anyone with a board to answer to. Benji doesn't want the drama. Benji's server, it turns out, already has forty members who are Desiree Holloway's recruits, organizing quietly, comparing notes, slowly realizing what they've been sold into — moderated, unpaid, with genuine skill, by **Sister Rosalind Okonjo**, who built the rules herself because her parish youth group needed somewhere safe to talk and nobody official got there first.

Desiree finds out about the drive within forty-eight hours — her operation has better internal security than three of the core seven's companies combined, which is its own kind of joke — and sends people to get it back by whatever means necessary, "necessary" defined generously. K-Dot, Benji, and Sister Rosalind end up needing a fourth set of skills fast: **Felicity Drummond-Asante**, the rival-dynasty former child star who has spent her entire adult life being underestimated by exactly the kind of people Desiree Holloway preys on, and who has her own reasons — a mother, briefly, disastrously "mentored" by Desiree years ago — for wanting this specific woman's empire to end.

None of them like each other much at first. K-Dot thinks Benji's too paranoid; Benji thinks K-Dot's a liability; Felicity thinks all of them are amateurs; Sister Rosalind quietly out-strategizes everyone in the room and says almost nothing about it. What starts as four people trying to survive a bad forty-eight hours becomes, slowly, over a genuinely funny and increasingly high-stakes few months, an actual found family — and a decision: they don't just leak the data and disappear. They build the server Sister Rosalind was already running, properly, permanently, into something bigger — a real home for the people Desiree preys on, run by nobody's board, safe from exactly the kind of institutional capture this franchise has spent nine films warning about.

Desiree tries everything — a buyout offer disguised as a "wellness partnership," a plant inside the beta, a threatened lawsuit, and finally, publicly, live at her own empire's biggest annual event, a live demonstration meant to discredit the whole platform as a scam run by "bitter has-beens and internet trolls." It backfires spectacularly: Felicity's own testimony, Sister Rosalind's calm, devastating moral clarity, Benji's technical proof, and K-Dot's genuinely undeniable charm turn Desiree's own stage into the site of her downline's mass, public, viral defection. THE OUTPOST launches publicly that same week — chaotic, funny, real, run by four people who didn't know each other five months ago and now, unmistakably, do. Desiree, per the Villains Bench's rules for THE SWARM, is not caught, not prosecuted, not finished — just rebranded, already testing a new name for the next version of the exact same grift, somewhere else, for someone else's franchise to eventually deal with.

## THE ANTAGONISTS

Per the Villains Bench rules (`TSU_Continuity_Bible.md` §5), and per the house style's explicit assignment of Guardians' slot to satire.

1. **DESIREE HOLLOWAY** *(THE SWARM's human face)* — a wellness-and-abundance mentorship mogul, genuinely magnetic, genuinely believes her own pitch some of the time, running a nine-figure pyramid scheme with real victims and real, extremely online charisma. Per the Villains Bench's own rule, she is never fully defeated — only rebranded, the same parasitic subculture reskinning itself the way it always has (per her category's bible entry: "bloggers -> YouTubers -> Instagram influencers -> TikTokers"; here, the wellness-guru-to-abundance-coach pipeline).
2. **THE ENGINE**, tenth manifestation, played almost entirely for comedy this time — THE OUTPOST's earliest server-recommendation system is hilariously, chaotically bad at matching people to communities, recommending Sister Rosalind's youth-group server to cryptocurrency day traders and vice versa, a rare full activation of the Villains Bench's mandate that satire is where THE ENGINE gets to be funny instead of frightening.
3. **DESIREE'S "WELLNESS SECURITY" TEAM** — not a single mastermind, a genuinely absurd, over-resourced private security apparatus built to protect a scented-candle empire, treated throughout as comic rather than menacing, in keeping with the house style's "dialed carefully" satire mandate.

## HOW THIS FILM EXPANDS THE FRANCHISE

- **A wholly new found family, exactly as the slot requires:** none of the core seven lead this film, mirroring Guardians' own refusal to reuse the existing team — the first time in ten films this franchise has done that.
- **Four dormant Wildcards, activated together on schedule:** K-Dot, Sister Rosalind, and Felicity were all introduced in `TSU_Extended_Ensemble.md` explicitly to "pull the world outward into a different subculture the founders wouldn't otherwise touch" — this is the film where all three finally get used at once, alongside Benji's continuing arc.
- **THE SWARM's first true activation:** present in the Villains Bench since the Platform Roster's earliest draft, never dramatized until now — and, per its own rules, deliberately left uncaught.
- **The house style's satire mandate, finally cashed in:** `TSU_Continuity_Bible.md` §2 named this exact tonal register for this exact narrative need years of in-universe continuity before this film existed.
- **A genuine tonal breath before Release Phase 2's finale:** the found-family levity here is deliberate contrast heading into whatever ensemble-scale stakes Film 11 (the Age of Ultron slot) will require.
