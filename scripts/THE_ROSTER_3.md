# THE ROSTER 3: THE UNSEEN
### TSU Film 7 — Release Phase 2, Film 1 — the "Iron Man 3" release slot

---

## THE PITCH

**Platform:** THE ROSTER, a third time. Not a new founding story, same as `scripts/THE_ROSTER_2.md` wasn't — this is what happens to the same man and the same company six months after the worst public crisis either of them has ever survived.

**Why a third ROSTER film fits the Iron Man 3 slot exactly:** Iron Man 3 opens on a hero who helped save the world in a giant public battle and is now quietly falling apart from the inside — panic attacks, obsessive over-building, a past mistake finally arriving to collect. Marc-Anthony Bullock stood on a stage in front of the world's press six months ago and watched a rival's platform vaporize live on television. That's not nothing to walk away from. This film is the bill for that night.

**Who this film belongs to:** Marc-Anthony Bullock, again — but not alone this time, which is the actual point. Esther Smilley, now running much of THE ROSTER's operations per the locked status coming out of `scripts/THE_ASSEMBLY.md`, gets her own full hero beat here rather than a supporting one, mirroring Pepper Potts's Extremis-era arc in the source film exactly: she is not rescued, she rescues herself, using her own competence rather than borrowed power. Amond Baker, Tyrese Avery, and Arianna Cummings all appear as a real, coordinated ensemble — this is the first ROSTER film where Marc-Anthony is never expected to solve anything by himself, and the plot is built to prove that's the correct way to run a company and a life.

**In-universe timeline:** **January 5, 2012 – May 10, 2012.** Opens on Marc-Anthony's 30th birthday (per `TSU_Core_Cast.md`, b. Jan 5, 1982) and closes on the fixed, locked date of Emma and Victoria Bullock's birth (`TSU_Family_Bible.md`) — a five-month span deliberately bookended by two dates that were already canon before this film existed. Esther (b. Sept 20, 1982) is 29, visibly, heavily pregnant with twins conceived the previous September (`scripts/THE_ASSEMBLY.md` scene 32) for the entire runtime — her physical state is not incidental to the plot, it's the whole reason the climax has to be won without anyone laying a hand on her.

## LOGLINE

Six months after watching an empire built on speed collapse live on television, Marc-Anthony Bullock can't sleep, can't stop building, and can't tell his very pregnant partner the truth about what the stress is doing to his heart — until a masked figure calling itself THE UNSEEN starts proving, one convincing fake video at a time, that THE ROSTER can no longer tell its real users from manufactured ones, and the only person who can actually stop it isn't him.

## SYNOPSIS

It's January 2012. Marc-Anthony Bullock turns 30 the same week THE ROSTER posts its best quarter ever, and neither fact makes him feel the way he expected. Since watching THE EVERYTHING collapse on a stage in front of the world, he hasn't stopped moving — a new verification feature every few weeks, a new security layer, a new safeguard against a catastrophe that already happened to someone else's company, not his. Esther Smilley, seven months pregnant with twins and effectively running the company day to day while he chases ghosts, is the only one who says the quiet part out loud: he's not preventing another Everything. He's having one, slowly, inside himself.

A masked figure calling itself **THE UNSEEN** starts releasing videos: verified THE ROSTER users, seemingly caught on camera saying and doing things they never actually said or did — flawless, undetectable, and spreading through a bot-amplified swarm of fake accounts that makes each one look like it's already everywhere before anyone can debunk it (per the Villains Bench, THE COPIES' latest and most dangerous form). The platform's entire premise — verified, real, trustworthy — starts to buckle in public, one viral fake at a time. Marc-Anthony, unable to sleep, unable to delegate, throws himself at finding THE UNSEEN personally, alienating Esther and worrying Amond in exactly the pattern of every crisis this franchise has already shown him surviving badly before he learns.

The reveal, when it comes, isn't a stranger. It's **Desmond Okafor-Lindqvist** — the founder who built a nearly identical verification-and-authenticity platform two years before THE ROSTER existed, pitched Marc-Anthony directly on licensing the underlying technology in the company's earliest days, and got turned down without much of a second thought. He's spent the years since watching THE ROSTER succeed with a worse version of his own idea, and THE UNSEEN isn't vengeance exactly — it's proof, delivered as cruelly as he can manage, that the technology he built to verify people is just as good at manufacturing them.

The attack turns personal when a devastatingly convincing fake — Esther, apparently caught saying something career-ending and cruel about the company's own users — gets released two weeks before her due date. Marc-Anthony's instinct is to handle it alone, quietly, even considering paying Desmond off through back channels rather than risk Esther's health with the stress of a public fight. Esther refuses to let him. Working with litigator **Constance Abiodun-Mayhew** and a cryptographic proof-of-origin system Tyrese and Arianna's engineering teams build in days, she stages her own public response — not a denial, a demonstration, live, of exactly how the fake was made and who made it — and dismantles Desmond's credibility herself, on her own terms, seven months pregnant and entirely unrescued.

Desmond is exposed, his network collapses, and the platform survives — not because Marc-Anthony finally beat the villain single-handedly, but because he finally let the people around him do the parts he couldn't. In the hospital on May 10, 2012, hours after Emma and Victoria are born, Marc-Anthony tells Esther the truth about his heart for the first time without her having to ask — and asks her to be THE ROSTER's President, formalizing the job she's already been doing, so that the company never again depends on one person refusing to put anything down.

## THE ANTAGONISTS

Per the Villains Bench rules (`TSU_Continuity_Bible.md` §5), the real cause traces to a character decision — here, one made years before the film even starts.

1. **DESMOND OKAFOR-LINDQVIST** *(returning from `TSU_Extended_Ensemble.md`, "The Rivals" — the Aldrich Killian analog)* — "the rival who was right about everything and still lost." His locked bible entry already called for him to resurface "as a bitter, brilliant consultant everyone secretly wants to hire and nobody wants to publicly credit." This film is that resurfacing, at its most dangerous: he doesn't want to be hired anymore. He wants the industry to feel, once, what it's like to not be able to tell what's real.
2. **THE UNSEEN** *(the Mandarin analog — a manufactured decoy, not a person)* — a persona, not a villain, built by Desmond specifically to be unmaskable: no manifesto that holds together, no consistent demands, because it was never meant to be a real threat with real motives, only a terrifying shape for the public and the press to aim their fear at while the actual attack happens underneath it.
3. **THE COPIES**, in their most dangerous form yet — the bot-amplification swarm that makes each fake video look pre-viral before a human ever sees it, the clearest possible illustration of the Villains Bench's own description: "dismissed as harmless until it isn't."
4. **THE ENGINE**, seventh manifestation — THE ROSTER's own trust-scoring verification algorithm, fooled completely by Desmond's synthetic media, forcing a full rebuild around real human relationships and cryptographic proof-of-origin rather than pattern-matching alone.

## HOW THIS FILM OPENS RELEASE PHASE 2

- **The first film of Release Phase 2, structurally honest about it:** where Release Phase 1 was about founding things, Release Phase 2 opens on the cost of having founded them — Marc-Anthony's arc here is explicitly about surviving success, not building it.
- **Desmond Okafor-Lindqvist, activated exactly as designed:** his bible entry's built-in "resurfaces in later Phases" clause gets its first real payoff.
- **Esther Smilley promoted from arc-adjacent to load-bearing:** she is not new to the story, but this is the first film where she is the one who wins the third act, not the one who watches Marc-Anthony win it.
- **The franchise's thesis, sharpened once more:** Films 1 through 6 asked who's real, who's heard, who's owed, who's credited, who's measured, and what happens when everyone has to unite. Film 7 asks the next honest question: once you've built the thing that verifies what's real, what happens the day someone builds a machine that's better at faking it than you are at catching it?
- **THE COPIES' first true activation:** present conceptually since the Platform Roster's original draft, never dramatized as an actual threat until now.
