# THE FRAME 3: THE ACCORD
### TSU Film 13 — Release Phase 3, Film 1 — the "Captain America: Civil War" release slot

---

## THE PITCH

**Platform:** Not a new founding story — the third THE FRAME film, reusing existing infrastructure the way `scripts/THE_ROSTER_3.md` and `scripts/THE_FRAME_2.md` did before it. No new core-seven platform assignment. The threat this time isn't a villain the ensemble can unite against — it's each other, and a piece of paper.

**Why this is Civil War, structurally:** Civil War is about the team turning on each other over a real, defensible disagreement that neither side is simply wrong about, catalyzed by a fresh mistake and detonated by an old grief nobody saw coming. TSU spent the entirety of `scripts/THE_ASSEMBLY_2.md` building exactly this fault line on purpose: Marc-Anthony Bullock, guilt-soaked over GENESIS, believes real oversight is the only responsible path forward; Arianna Cummings believes he's learning precisely the wrong lesson from his own disaster, and that no oversight body — government or founder-run — should hold unaccountable power over everyone else's work. `scripts/THE_EMBER.md`'s stinger already put them on opposite sides of Senator Ashworth-Okafor's proposed regulatory framework. This film is where that framework gets a name, a vote, and a body count of its own — the **Founders' Oversight Accord**.

**Who this film belongs to:** Arianna Cummings and Marc-Anthony Bullock, full co-leads, exactly as faithful to the source as a "Captain America" film that is functionally an Avengers film. Amond Baker, Esther Smilley, Tyrese Avery, Tyler Chapman, Priya Ramasubramanian, Marcus Nguyen-Brandt, Naledi Ferreira-Osei, and Benji Oyelaran-Cox all carry real, load-bearing arcs — not cameo texture. One new one-and-done character, **Solange Achebe-Marsh**, is introduced per house style's ensemble-event exception.

**In-universe timeline:** **January 2017 – September 2017**, closing out the tail end of Story Phase 3 (2010–2017) just before the timeline crosses into Story Phase 4. Ages verified against `TSU_Core_Cast.md`: Marc-Anthony and Amond turn 35 in January and February 2017; Tyrese is 35, turning 36 in July; Arianna, Esther, and Harmony are all 34, each turning 35 across the film's run; Tyler turned 35 on January 4, 2017, the day before Marc-Anthony, unbroken across thirteen films. Emma and Victoria Bullock turn 5 on May 10, 2017 — old enough now that Marc-Anthony's choices in this film are, for the first time, something they will eventually be able to ask him about directly.

## LOGLINE

When a Founders' Compact operation meant to stop a resurgent grift scheme causes real collateral harm to people who never signed up for any of it, a government-mandated Oversight Accord forces every platform founder to choose a side — and Marc-Anthony Bullock and Arianna Cummings, closest they've ever been to true equals, end up leading opposite ones, just as a grieving stranger with a real grievance and a patient plan starts making sure neither side ever finds a way back to the other.

## SYNOPSIS

It's January 2017. The Founders' Compact — Marc-Anthony, Arianna, Priya Ramasubramanian, Marcus Nguyen-Brandt, and Naledi Ferreira-Osei — has spent nine months being exactly what it was chartered to be: smaller, slower, and human, five people instead of one algorithm. It has also never been tested by anything bigger than a boardroom disagreement. That changes in a cold open: Kenji Adeyemi-Harald flags a Southeast Asian resurgence of THE SWARM — a wellness-and-abundance grift structurally identical to Desiree Holloway's, rebranded exactly per the Villains Bench's own rule — moving fast enough that the Compact authorizes an intervention before its usual full-consensus process finishes. Arianna, closest to the region operationally through THE FRAME's creator-payment infrastructure, moves a day early on her own judgment. It works, mostly — the grift collapses — but the account freezes she orders to stop it also lock out a working-class vendor network that had nothing to do with the scheme, wiping out months of income for real people with no appeal process and no warning, an uncomfortably exact echo of what GENESIS did to the founders themselves in `scripts/THE_ASSEMBLY_2.md`.

The story leads within days. Senator Gwendolyn Ashworth-Okafor's committee, already primed since the stinger of `scripts/THE_EMBER.md`, finalizes the **Founders' Oversight Accord**: any cross-platform safety tool or founder-run oversight body — a category that describes GENESIS, and, uncomfortably, describes the Compact itself — must register with a government-reviewable board before it can act. Marc-Anthony signs immediately, publicly, urging the others to follow; his position hasn't changed since `scripts/THE_ASSEMBLY_2.md`, and this film gives him a fresh, specific, defensible reason to still believe it. Amond, carrying real guilt of his own about Kwabena Ferreira-Osei, signs alongside him, reluctantly. Naledi signs too — not out of institutional trust, but because she has spent nine months believing more real accountability, not less, is the only thing that would have honored what her brother died trying to build. Esther signs for THE ROSTER's board. Arianna refuses, for THE FRAME, on the same grounds she held in Tyler Chapman's living room: no single body, elected or not, should hold this much power over everyone else's independent judgment, and an Accord written in the direct aftermath of a disaster is exactly the wrong moment to write it calmly. Tyler Chapman, who spent `scripts/THE_EMBER.md` learning firsthand what losing control of something you built actually costs, sides with her immediately. Marcus Nguyen-Brandt, whose entire redemption arc has been about refusing unaccountable power a second time, sides with her too. Priya, torn, ultimately sides with due process over blanket oversight.

Into this fracture walks **Solange Achebe-Marsh** — a data-verification analyst, unremarkable, precise, whose husband was one of the unnamed casualties in the wider evacuation chaos at the Atlanta fulfillment hub during GENESIS's collapse, a death the franchise never gave a name until now. She has spent nine months building a case, methodically, not for violence but for exposure: doctored server logs, spoofed to match Benji Oyelaran-Cox's well-documented THE VERDICT-era technical fingerprint, framing Benji for sabotaging a Compact-monitored system out of resentment at having been "forgiven too easily" the first time. It's not a wild accusation — it's built entirely from true, public facts, arranged in exactly the wrong order, the same method that made GENESIS's own diagnosis so hard to simply dismiss. Under the new Accord, Marc-Anthony's side is legally obligated to detain Benji for review. Arianna, who personally vouched for Benji's redemption after `scripts/THE_FRAME_2.md`, refuses to let that happen without due process, and shields them instead.

The confrontation lands at a cross-platform industry summit — TSU's airport, the whole ensemble converging at once, legal injunctions instead of punches, a chase through a server floor instead of a runway, Priya's team running interference against Esther's compliance officers, Tyler and Marcus running technical countermeasures against Amond's security detail — genuinely funny in places, genuinely painful in others, exactly the tonal balance this house style has always protected. In the chaos, Amond is seriously injured — not by Solange, not by any villain, but by friendly-fire-equivalent chaos between the two founder factions themselves, a server rack crushing his leg during a stampede neither side intended to cause. It's the first time in thirteen films this franchise has let its own internal conflict, rather than an outside force, be the direct cause of real harm to one of its own.

Solange, cornered, doesn't run and doesn't gloat. She tells Marc-Anthony the one piece of truth she saved for last, privately, cruelly: the specific redundant fail-safe that would have prevented her husband's death was cut from GENESIS's build in its final week, over Amond's objection, because Marc-Anthony wanted to hit a launch date. It has nothing to do with the Accord and everything to do with a choice he made and never disclosed. It breaks something in him that the entire ideological argument never touched. The person who actually stops the standoff from turning into something worse is **Naledi Ferreira-Osei** — Kwabena's twin, the one person in the room with the standing to say she lost the same thing Solange did and chose a different road with it. She doesn't defend the Accord to Solange. She just sits down next to her and grieves out loud, honestly, for the first time either woman has been allowed to since April 2016. Solange surrenders. Nobody frames it as a victory.

The film doesn't end on reconciliation. The Accord passes, ratified by a majority of the industry's major boards, with THE ROSTER and THE WIRE's signatures on it. THE FRAME remains the one major platform operating outside it, by Arianna's choice, a real and lasting institutional split rather than a plot device that resolves by the credits. Benji is cleared, but shaken enough to step back from public-facing work entirely for a while, quietly protected at THE OUTPOST's infrastructure — the platform they helped found now the place that shields them, a small, deliberate, full-circle image. Amond survives, will walk again, and tells Marc-Anthony, from a hospital bed, the truest thing anyone says in the whole film: that he doesn't actually think Marc-Anthony was wrong to want oversight — he thinks Marc-Anthony was wrong, the same way he's always been wrong, to decide something that big alone and tell people about it after. Marc-Anthony and Arianna part on the tarmac, still friends, still not on the same side of anything that matters right now, an ending that is honest about costing something real rather than pretending a hug fixes it. The stinger doesn't tease a villain. It's Senator Ashworth-Okafor's committee, six weeks later, quietly opening a file on THE FRAME's continued non-compliance — the Accord isn't finished with Arianna, even if this film is.

## THE ANTAGONISTS

Per the Villains Bench rules (`TSU_Continuity_Bible.md` §5), the real cause traces to character decisions on both sides — this film deliberately has no single villain either side can unite against, which is the entire point of the slot.

1. **SOLANGE ACHEBE-MARSH**, new, one-and-done per house style's ensemble-event exception — not a mastermind seeking power, a grieving widow seeking exposure rather than violence, methodical in exactly the way this franchise's best rivals always are: she never lies, she just arranges true things in the worst possible order, the same technique that made GENESIS's own diagnosis so hard to dismiss in `scripts/THE_ASSEMBLY_2.md`. Not defeated in a confrontation — surrenders, once met with Naledi Ferreira-Osei's grief instead of anyone's anger, consistent with the franchise's established pattern of extending real mercy rather than manufacturing a clean villain defeat.
2. **THE SWARM**, resurging in Southeast Asia exactly per its bible's own rule — never fully defeated, only rebranded — functioning here purely as the inciting incident rather than the film's actual antagonist; its collapse is the accident that starts everything, not the crisis itself.
3. **The Founders' Oversight Accord** itself is not a villain — it's a real, defensible policy response to a real disaster, deliberately written so that neither Marc-Anthony's nor Arianna's position is simply correct. The actual cause of this film's harm is not the Accord's existence but the speed and secrecy with which both a Compact intervention (Arianna's) and a critical safety cut (Marc-Anthony's, on GENESIS, disclosed too late) were each made — character decisions, exactly as the Bench's founding rule requires, not an abstract institutional failure.

## HOW THIS FILM CARRIES THE FRANCHISE FORWARD

- **Cashes in the rift `scripts/THE_ASSEMBLY_2.md` planted on purpose:** Marc-Anthony and Arianna's disagreement was never meant to resolve quietly — this film gives it a name, a vote, and a real, lasting institutional consequence, opening Release Phase 3 on division rather than a defeated villain.
- **Gives Naledi Ferreira-Osei her first real test as a Founders' Compact member:** her choice to sign the Accord, for reasons entirely her own and entirely sympathetic, refuses to let her be read as simply "on Marc-Anthony's side" — and her final scene with Solange is the film's actual emotional climax, not the summit standoff.
- **The franchise's first internally-caused serious injury:** Amond's injury, caused by the founders' own fracture rather than any villain, is a deliberate escalation of the pattern this franchise has run since Desmond Cummings Jr.'s Film 5 injury — consequences that vary in cause and stay permanent rather than a repeated beat.
- **THE FRAME becomes the franchise's first platform operating outside a major regulatory framework by choice** — a standing piece of infrastructure future films can return to, escalate, or resolve, the same way the Founders' Compact itself became standing infrastructure after Film 11.
- **Closes Story Phase 3 on an open institutional wound**, positioning Release Phase 3's next films to either widen or heal the Accord split as Story Phase 4 (2018–present) begins.
