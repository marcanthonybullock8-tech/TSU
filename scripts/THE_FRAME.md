# THE FRAME
### TSU Film 5 — Release Phase 1 — the "Captain America: The First Avenger" release slot

---

## THE PITCH

**Platform:** THE FRAME — TSU's fictional 1:1 counterpart to Instagram (per `TSU_Platform_Roster.md`, Phase 2 — "Origin as a bare-bones check-in app that got reduced to just photos + filters — addition by subtraction... became the platonic ideal by stripping everything down to its purest form.") This platform was flagged as reserved for exactly this slot back in `scripts/THE_PROJECTOR.md`'s canon updates (`TSU_Continuity_Bible.md` §7 Q1) — it carried the Captain America framing before any release-slot planning even reached it.

**Why THE FRAME for the Captain America slot:** Cap's whole story is a person told, over and over, by every gatekeeper in the room that he doesn't have what it takes — not for lack of merit, for lack of the superficial thing they're actually measuring — until someone finally sees what's real underneath and gives him a fair shot. Of the remaining unassigned core seven, only one has that exact wound already locked into canon as mandatory to dramatize: `TSU_Continuity_Bible.md` §4 flags Arianna Cummings's origin wound — "lost role to follower count" — as something that "must be dramatized, not just referenced." It already was, once, as the spark for someone *else's* company in Film 1. This film is the one where it becomes hers.

**Who this film belongs to:** Arianna Cummings, solo founder — no co-founder-betrayal structure this time (that trope already ran twice, in `scripts/THE_ROSTER.md` and implicitly through Amond's arc; Cap's actual antagonist is external, not a best friend, and this film follows suit). Malcolm Pierce, a new character, fills the Peggy Carter position — competent, unglamorous, sees her clearly before anyone else does. Her brother Desmond Cummings Jr. (`TSU_Family_Bible.md`, already locked as a firefighter) fills the Bucky position. Dr. Adaeze Okonkwo-Brennan (`TSU_Extended_Ensemble.md`) fills the Erskine position — the one person who takes her raw idea seriously when every investor calls it "not flashy enough."

**In-universe timeline:** **January 2009 – October 25, 2010.** This film is **Release Phase 1, Film 5** — that's the number that matters when discussing "the sixth film" or "which Phase this film is." Its *story*, separately, falls inside **Story Phase 2** on the in-universe platform-launch timeline (`TSU_Continuity_Bible.md` §3: THE FRAME's launch is the anchor that closes Story Phase 2), the same way *Captain America: The First Avenger* is a Release Phase 1 film whose story is set decades before Release Phase 1's other entries. See `TSU_Continuity_Bible.md` §9 for the full Release-Phase-vs-Story-Phase distinction. This script locks the exact launch date to October 25, 2010: Arianna's 28th birthday. Ages verified against `TSU_Core_Cast.md`: Arianna (b. Oct 25, 1982) is 26 at the film's open, turning 27 in October 2009 mid-film, and 28 the day THE FRAME launches — the industry spent years making her birthday a reminder of what hadn't happened yet; this is the first one that means something different.

## LOGLINE

For five years, Arianna Cummings has heard the same verdict in a hundred different rooms: the most talented person there, and never the one who gets the part. When she finally stops asking an industry that keeps grading her on the wrong scale for permission, she builds a platform that measures exactly one thing — the work itself — and finds out the hard way that the people profiting off insecurity would very much like her to fail.

## SYNOPSIS

Five years after losing a role to a stranger with a bigger online following, Arianna Cummings, 26, has stopped counting the near-misses because the number stopped being useful information a while ago. She's still the most talented person in every room, per the industry's own private consensus, and still not the one anyone actually calls back. A chance encounter with **Malcolm Pierce**, a documentary photographer shooting a piece on working actors nobody's heard of, turns into something else — first a real conversation about what actually gets seen and why, then a partnership, then more. Malcolm doesn't have a following either. He has an eye. Together they start sketching an idea: a platform built entirely around the work — a real audition tape, a real headshot, a real four-minute monologue — with no follower count visible anywhere on the page, because the whole point is to stop letting that number do the deciding.

Every investor they pitch says some version of the same thing: it's not flashy enough, it won't grow fast enough, nobody wants "authentic," they want aspirational. **Dr. Adaeze Okonkwo-Brennan**, the Stanford professor who's heard forty founders explain their idea badly before they're ready, is the only person who takes it seriously — not because it's polished, but because it's the first pitch in months actually trying to solve a real problem instead of chase a trend. Her one introduction gets them just enough seed money to start building.

Meanwhile, **Celeste Marlowe** — a beauty-and-image industry veteran who has spent two decades monetizing exactly the insecurity Arianna's platform is built to undercut — is building something else entirely, backed by investor **Delphine Okonkwo-Ash**: a filter-and-rating app that lets users algorithmically "perfect" their photos and scores them against each other, engineered from day one to be maximally addictive and maximally profitable off the same comparison culture that's been grinding Arianna down her whole career. Celeste isn't a cartoon; she genuinely believes she's giving people what they actually want. That certainty is what makes her dangerous.

The two platforms launch into the same crowded season. Celeste's grows faster, louder, glossier — real numbers, real hype, real pressure on Arianna and Malcolm to add filters, add rankings, add exactly the mechanics they built THE FRAME to refuse. The strain nearly breaks them. It's Arianna's brother, **Desmond Cummings Jr.**, pulled from a structure fire mid-shift with third-degree burns during the same week THE FRAME is supposed to launch, who forces the film's real reckoning — not through a scene of him giving a speech, but simply by surviving, badly hurt, in a hospital bed, while Arianna has to decide in real time whether the platform still matters enough to launch on schedule or whether nothing does right now except her family.

She launches anyway, on her own terms, on her own birthday, from a hospital waiting room — a quiet, unglamorous, real launch that undersells itself completely and still finds exactly the audience it was built for: actors, photographers, and craftspeople who've spent their whole careers being told the wrong thing was being measured. THE FRAME doesn't beat Celeste's app on day one, or day thirty. It doesn't need to. It's still there a year later, still growing, still exactly what it was built to be, while Celeste's platform starts quietly generating the exact kind of user-harm headlines that were always going to catch up to it. The film ends with Arianna, finally, unmistakably, the one who gets the part — except now she's the one deciding who gets cast.

## THE ANTAGONISTS

Per the Villains Bench rules (`TSU_Continuity_Bible.md` §5), the real cause traces to character decisions.

1. **CELESTE MARLOWE** *(the Red Skull / HYDRA analog)* — a beauty-and-image industry veteran, genuinely brilliant, who has built her entire career on monetizing the same comparison culture that wounded Arianna, and believes without irony that she's serving a real human desire rather than exploiting one. Backed by **Delphine Okonkwo-Ash** (`TSU_Extended_Ensemble.md`, "The Other Money" — her locked bible entry as someone "constantly trying to rewrite what actually happened in rooms she was in" pays off directly here). Not defeated in a confrontation — outlasted, the same way HYDRA's ideology outlives the fight in the first film.
2. **THE ENGINE**, fifth manifestation — appearing here as an aesthetic-amplification bias: an early build of THE FRAME's own discovery feed quietly favors heavily filtered images over unedited ones, the platform almost becoming a smaller version of exactly what it was built to reject. Arianna and Malcolm catch it and rebuild the ranking logic around unedited engagement before launch — this film's version of "the serum works because of who takes it, not the serum itself."
3. **THE INDUSTRY'S OWN GATEKEEPING**, again — per house style, the true antagonist across TSU's founding stories is never a single villain but the system itself; Celeste is simply its most literal, most profitable expression.

## HOW THIS FILM CARRIES THE FRANCHISE FORWARD

- **Closes a five-film-old loop:** Arianna's wound, locked as mandatory-to-dramatize back in Film 1, finally gets to be about her own founding story instead of someone else's origin spark.
- **The "who gets credit / who gets seen" thesis, completed for now:** Film 1 verified who's real, Film 2 who gets heard, Film 3 a buried debt, Film 4 who gets credited, and now Film 5 — who gets *measured*, and by what. Five films, five angles on the same argument.
- **THE FRAME's fixed anchor honored exactly:** launches 2010, closing Phase 2 per `TSU_Continuity_Bible.md` §3, with no timeline changes required.
- **Two dormant recurring characters activated on schedule:** Dr. Adaeze Okonkwo-Brennan and Delphine Okonkwo-Ash were both flagged in `TSU_Extended_Ensemble.md` as 30%-bucket cross-Phase characters waiting for their moment — this is that moment for both.
- **The clearest "Avengers Initiative" tease yet:** Priya Ramasubramanian's corkboard (`scripts/THE_ROSTER_2.md`, `scripts/THE_PROJECTOR.md`) gets a fourth card, and for the first time says the quiet part out loud (see stinger).

## SAMPLE PAGES

```
                                                              THE FRAME

                                                        Written by [WRITER]

FADE IN:

INT. AUDITION WAITING ROOM — DAY

SUPER: "JANUARY 2009"

Folding chairs. Fluorescent lights. A dozen actors clutching
headshots like exam results. ARIANNA CUMMINGS, 26, sits with
the specific stillness of someone who has done this exact
thing several hundred times and stopped expecting the outcome
to be different.

A CASTING ASSISTANT calls a name. Not hers. An actress with a
ring light balanced on her knee, checking a phone with a
notification count too large to be real, gets up instead.

Across the room, MALCOLM PIERCE, 30s, unassuming, a camera
around his neck that's seen more use than his jacket, has been
quietly photographing the room for the last twenty minutes.
He catches Arianna watching him.

                    MALCOLM
          Sorry. Occupational habit. You looked
          like you were doing math in your head.

                    ARIANNA
          I was counting. This is forty-one.

                    MALCOLM
          Forty-one what?

                    ARIANNA
          Rooms like this one. I stopped counting
          the almosts a while back. Started
          counting the rooms instead. Different
          number, same math.

Malcolm lifts the camera, a question in the gesture. Arianna
nods, almost surprising herself. He takes the shot — not a
posed one, just her, mid-thought, unguarded.

                    MALCOLM
          For what it's worth, that's the most
          interesting face I've photographed all
          week, and I've been here since eight
          this morning.

                    ARIANNA
          (dry, the first real smile of the scene)
          Interesting doesn't get you the part.

                    MALCOLM
          No. It really doesn't. That's sort of my
          whole problem with the part.

                                                              CUT TO:
```

```
INT. ARIANNA'S APARTMENT — NIGHT                    [p. 40]

Late. A laptop between them, contact sheets spread across the
coffee table. MALCOLM scrolls through an early build of THE
FRAME's discovery feed — a grid of uploaded reels and photos.

                    MALCOLM
          (slowing down, frowning)
          Something's off. Look what's floating to
          the top.

He clicks through the top ten. Every single one has been
visibly, heavily filtered — smoothed skin, brightened eyes,
nothing that looks like an actual audition tape anymore.

                    ARIANNA
          That's not — we didn't build a ranking
          system that cares about that.

                    MALCOLM
          We built a ranking system that cares
          about engagement. And it turns out
          engagement cares about filtered faces,
          same as everywhere else. We didn't
          decide this. The math did, on its own,
          because we let the math decide.

                    ARIANNA
          (quiet, genuinely rattled)
          We built the exact thing we hated.

                    MALCOLM
          We built the first draft of it. That's
          different from building it on purpose.
          The question is whether we notice in
          time to actually fix it.

Arianna is already pulling the laptop toward her, sleeves
rolled up.

                    ARIANNA
          We have time. We haven't launched
          anything yet.

                                                              CUT TO:

```
INT. HOSPITAL WAITING ROOM — NIGHT           [low point, p. 91]

ARIANNA sits alone, a bag at her feet with a laptop and a
folder of launch materials she hasn't opened in six hours.
PATRICIA CUMMINGS sits beside her, quiet, having run out of
things to say that help.

                    PATRICIA
          You should go home. Sleep. He's stable.

                    ARIANNA
          I'm not going anywhere.

                    PATRICIA
          (gently)
          I know. I'm not really telling you to.

A long silence. Arianna looks at the folder. Doesn't open it.

                    ARIANNA
          I was supposed to launch tomorrow. On my
          birthday. I picked the date on purpose,
          made it mean something, and now I can't
          even remember why it felt important.

                    PATRICIA
          (careful, honest)
          It's allowed to still matter, Arianna.
          Your brother getting hurt doesn't erase
          the five years before this week.

                    ARIANNA
          It feels like it should.

                    PATRICIA
          It doesn't work that way. Nothing's ever
          worked that way for you. You've always
          had to hold two true things at the same
          time and keep walking anyway.

Arianna doesn't answer. But her hand finally moves toward the
folder, just resting on top of it, not opening it yet.

                                                              CUT TO:

```
INT. HOSPITAL — DAY                                       [p. 104]

DESMOND CUMMINGS JR., bandaged, hooked to monitors, very much
awake and very much himself despite it all. ARIANNA sits
beside the bed.

                    DESMOND
          (voice rough, but the teasing fully intact)
          Why do you look like somebody died. I'm

                    ARIANNA
          I didn't launch. I couldn't. Not with you

                    DESMOND
          (genuinely exasperated)
          Ari. I have spent our entire lives

                    ARIANNA
          That's not remotely the same thing.

                    DESMOND
          It's exactly the same thing. It's just

          Go do yours.

                    ARIANNA
          You're insufferable.

                    DESMOND
          I got burned saving a guy's dog, Ari. I

```
INT. HOSPITAL WAITING ROOM — OCTOBER 25, 2010    [climax, p. 106]

ARIANNA, alone in a corner of the waiting room, laptop open,
launch page loaded. No party. No press. MALCOLM sits beside
her, having driven straight here the moment she called.

                    MALCOLM
          You don't have to make it a thing. It can
          just be a thing that happens.

                    ARIANNA
          (looking at the screen, then at him)
          It's my birthday and my brother's in a
          hospital bed forty feet from here and I'm
          about to launch a company from a plastic
          chair. This is, objectively, not how I
          pictured this.

                    MALCOLM
          How did you picture it?

                    ARIANNA
          (small, honest laugh)
          Bigger. Shinier. Somebody else's
          approval in the room somewhere.

                    MALCOLM
          And now?

Arianna looks at the screen a long moment. Hits the button.

                    ARIANNA
          Now it's just true. That's actually
          better.
